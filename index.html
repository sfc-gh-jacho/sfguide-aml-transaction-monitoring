Overview

From Half a Million Transactions to a Dozen Cases

A financial-crime team at a fictional bank reviews 90 days of activity: 500,000 transactions across 3,000 accounts. A dozen of them form classic money-laundering patterns. Here you build transparent rules that find them, score them and route each one to the right investigator.

A

Structuring

Cash split into deposits just under a reporting threshold to avoid a report.

B

Rapid movement

Large wires in, then nearly all of it out again within 48 hours. The account is a pass-through.

C

Funnel account

Many unrelated senders pay in, and the money leaves together in one large wire.

D

High-risk geography

Round-amount wires to destinations in a high-risk country tier.

Prerequisites

Before You Begin

  • Enterprise Edition or higher for the row access policy (Step 11). The detection rules and Dynamic Table work on any edition.
  • Cortex AI functions for the optional Step 12. The script uses llama3.3-70b. If it isn't available in your region, swap in another model or enable cross-region inference.
  • A role to run it. ACCOUNTADMIN is simplest in a sandbox. For regulated environments, the top of Step 1 includes a least-privilege option: an administrator creates AML_DEMO_ROLE once with only CREATE DATABASE, CREATE WAREHOUSE, CREATE ROLE and the SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER database role. You then set demo_role to that role on the first line of the script.
  • One Snowsight worksheet, run top to bottom. The script creates its own AML_DEMO_DB and AML_WH and changes nothing else.

Step 1

Setup

Choose the role that runs the guide, then create a dedicated database and an XSMALL warehouse that suspends after 60 seconds idle. Everything the guide builds lives inside these objects.

2
Step 2

Settings, Countries, Customers and Accounts

Rule thresholds live in an AML_SETTINGS table, so analysts can tune them without editing code. The reporting threshold is jurisdiction-specific, so set your own. Customers are IDs only, each with a KYC risk rating and a region. Countries are coded CTRY_A–CTRY_L so no real jurisdiction is labeled high risk.

3
Step 3

90 Days of Transactions

This step generates 500,000 card, cash, ACH and wire transactions deterministically. Normal activity is deliberately clean: cash deposits stay under $4,000, wires are at most $20,000, and international wires only go to low- or medium-risk countries. Any alert later therefore traces back to a planted case.

Step 5 · Rule A

Structuring

The rule finds three or more cash deposits within 7 days, each within 10% below the reporting threshold. A self-join over a 7-day window counts the near-threshold deposits, and QUALIFY keeps the strongest window per account. The threshold and band come from the settings table.

6
Step 6 · Rule B

Rapid Movement of Funds

For every large incoming wire, the rule sums the outgoing wires in the next 48 hours. If at least 90% of the money left again, the account behaves like a pass-through. Each explanation gives the amounts, the percentage and how many hours it took.

Step 7 · Rule C

Funnel Accounts

The rule looks back 7 days from every wire out of $25,000 or more. It flags the wire when ten or more different senders paid in at least $50,000 and a single outbound wire moved at least half of it on.

Step 8 · Rule D

High-risk Geography

This is the simplest rule: wires of $10,000 or more, in round thousands, to a country in the high-risk tier. On its own it's a weak signal. Combined with KYC risk in the scoring step, it helps prioritize.

9
Step 9

Risk Scoring and a Live Alert Queue

Each alert gets a 0–100 score: the rule's weight, plus the customer's KYC risk, plus the size of the money involved. The queue is a Dynamic Table, so it refreshes itself within a minute of new transactions landing. The script then adds a new structuring pattern, which appears after the next refresh.

Not a performance claim. The 12 alerts are the 12 cases deliberately planted in synthetic data, and normal activity was generated so it can't trip any rule. On real data these rules will produce false positives and need tuning, backtesting and model validation, as any monitoring program does.

Step 11 · Enterprise

Region-based Investigator Access

A row access policy on the alert queue reads a mapping table of role to region. East and West investigators see only their own customers, and the manager sees both. Adding an investigator or a region is an INSERT, not a policy rewrite.

Step 12 · Optional · Cortex AI

AI-drafted Case Summaries

AI_COMPLETE drafts a neutral, three-sentence case summary for each of the five highest-risk alerts, using only the facts in the alert. The prompt tells the model not to speculate, not to conclude a crime occurred and not to invent names. An investigator reviews and edits the draft. The model doesn't make the decision.

Step 13

Clean Up

Wrap-up

Key Takeaways

You built four AML typology rules, a scored and self-refreshing alert queue, region-based investigator access and AI-assisted case drafting, all inside Snowflake.

Transparent rules

Every rule is readable SQL, and every alert explains in plain English why it fired. That makes it easy to review, tune and defend.

Tunable without code

Thresholds live in a settings table, so analysts change them with an UPDATE, not a deployment.

Always current

A Dynamic Table re-scores alerts within a minute of new transactions, with no orchestration to maintain.

AI assists, people decide

Cortex drafts case summaries from the alert's facts, and investigators review and own the outcome.
